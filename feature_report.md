# Thin-file repayment-risk features and Home Credit data coverage

## Scope

This report identifies feature families used or discussed for consumer-loan underwriting when applicants have limited conventional credit history, then maps each family to the supplied Home Credit data. It is a design and data-availability assessment only: **no features were calculated and no EDA was performed**.

The supplied data consists of:

- `application_train`: 307,511 labeled current-loan applications; one row per unique `SK_ID_CURR`.
- `application_test`: 48,744 unlabeled current-loan applications; one row per unique `SK_ID_CURR`.
- The two tables have the same predictor schema. `TARGET` is present only in `application_train`, and their `SK_ID_CURR` populations do not overlap.

Here, **buildable** means the raw columns needed to define a feature are present. It does not mean that the feature is legally appropriate, validated, sufficiently complete, or ready for production use.

## What lenders and researchers use for thin-file applicants

Federal banking regulators emphasize borrower-specific alternative data that directly measures repayment capacity—especially permissioned bank-account cash flow, recurring income, expenses, and residual balances. They state that such information can help evaluate consumers who may not obtain mainstream credit and can be particularly useful when income comes from varied sources rather than one job. See the [Interagency Statement on the Use of Alternative Data in Credit Underwriting](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_interagency-statement_alternative-data.pdf).

Traditional and fintech underwriting also uses income, debt-to-income ratio, employment tenure, homeownership, loan amount, recent credit inquiries, credit scores/history, and local economic conditions. Jagtiani and Lemieux document these variables in LendingClub underwriting research and find that the platform's broader grades added predictive information beyond FICO alone; the article was published in *Financial Management* (DOI [10.1111/fima.12295](https://doi.org/10.1111/fima.12295); an accessible [Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia manuscript](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/working-papers/2018/wp18-15R.pdf) contains the variable definitions).

Lender disclosures show use of education and employment information alongside conventional credit and income data. Upstart's SEC filing says it attempts to verify applicant income, employment, and education information; see its [2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1647639/000164763925000018/upst-20241231.htm). Upstart also publicly describes income, employment status and tenure, education/professional background, non-W-2 income, local cost of living, and financial habits as possible model inputs; see its [underwriting disclosure](https://www.upstart.com/learn/how-upstart-works/).

Peer-reviewed evidence also shows that online-registration and access data—digital footprints—can add default-prediction information and complement bureau scores. See Berg, Burg, Gombović, and Puri, “On the Rise of FinTechs: Credit Scoring Using Digital Footprints,” *Review of Financial Studies* 33(7), DOI [10.1093/rfs/hhz099](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhz099).

These inputs remain subject to consumer-protection requirements. The CFPB states that creditors using complex models must give specific and accurate principal reasons for adverse action; see [CFPB Circular 2023-03](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_adverse_action_notice_circular_2023-09.pdf). Regulation B also restricts the use of protected characteristics and prohibits considering sex or marital status in credit evaluation, with limited rules governing age; see [12 CFR § 1002.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/subpart-A/section-1002.6).

## Feature-by-feature data map

Unless a row says otherwise, the listed columns exist in **both** `application_train` and `application_test`.

| Repayment-risk feature | Why lenders use it / evidence | Can this data build it? | Exact tables and columns, or missing requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stated income level | Income is a core measure of repayment capacity and is among the traditional risk controls documented by Jagtiani and Lemieux. | **Yes, directly** | `application_train.AMT_INCOME_TOTAL`, `application_test.AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` |
| Income source or employment status | Source and probable continuity of income help distinguish salaried, self-employed, pension, maternity-leave, and other income situations. The interagency statement specifically notes varied income sources, while Upstart discloses employment status and non-W-2 income as inputs. | **Yes, partially** | `NAME_INCOME_TYPE` in both tables identifies the reported income type. `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` gives the amount. The data has no pay-frequency field, employer-verified earnings series, tax-return history, or gig-income transaction history, so stability cannot be observed directly. |
| Employment tenure | Longer or stable employment is a conventional capacity/stability signal documented in the LendingClub study and Upstart disclosures. | **Yes, with a documented data-quality rule required** | `DAYS_EMPLOYED` in both tables can represent tenure relative to application. `OCCUPATION_TYPE` and `ORGANIZATION_TYPE` provide job and employer categories. `data_map.md` identifies `365243` as an undocumented positive sentinel, so it must not be interpreted as literal tenure. |
| Occupation and employer/industry type | Some lenders use profession, occupation, or employer information as part of income stability or earning-trajectory assessment. | **Yes, directly, but high compliance sensitivity** | `OCCUPATION_TYPE`, `ORGANIZATION_TYPE` in both tables. CFPB Circular 2023-03 specifically warns that if profession drives an adverse action, the actual specific reason must be disclosed; proxy and fair-lending testing would be necessary. |
| Full debt-to-income ratio (DTI) | DTI measures existing recurring debt obligations relative to income and is a standard LendingClub control. | **No** | The data has `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` but no total existing monthly debt payment, total debt balance, or complete liability schedule. `AMT_ANNUITY` is the annuity for the loan being applied for, not all pre-existing debt. Therefore a true DTI cannot be constructed. |
| Proposed-loan payment burden | Lenders compare the new payment with income to assess whether the requested obligation is affordable. | **Yes, conceptually** | `AMT_ANNUITY` and `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` in both tables supply the needed raw inputs. No ratio is computed in this report. This would be a proposed-payment-to-income feature, **not** full DTI. |
| Requested loan size relative to income or goods value | Loan amount and requested terms affect exposure and payment burden; loan amount appears among LendingClub risk controls. | **Yes, conceptually** | `AMT_CREDIT`, `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL`, `AMT_GOODS_PRICE`, `AMT_ANNUITY`, and `NAME_CONTRACT_TYPE` in both tables. No maturity, APR, interest rate, origination fee, or explicit loan-purpose column is present. |
| Loan term, APR, and price of credit | Maturity and rate affect scheduled payment and borrower burden; the LendingClub research explicitly includes rate and maturity. | **No** | Neither table contains contractual term/maturity, APR, nominal rate, or fee fields. `AMT_ANNUITY` does not uniquely recover term or rate. |
| Conventional credit score | Bureau scores summarize prior reported credit performance, but thin-file applicants may lack a usable score. | **Only as undocumented proxies** | `EXT_SOURCE_1`, `EXT_SOURCE_2`, and `EXT_SOURCE_3` appear in both tables as normalized external-source scores. The dictionary does not identify the provider, model, scale semantics, inputs, or whether any is a bureau score. They cannot responsibly be labeled FICO or a conventional bureau score. |
| Credit-file depth and age | Number and age of tradelines, oldest account, and mix of revolving/installment credit help distinguish truly thin files and measure experience. | **No** | No tradeline-level bureau table, account counts, oldest-account date, utilization, credit-limit, or credit-mix fields are supplied. The data therefore cannot even define a thin-file flag from bureau depth. |
| Prior delinquencies, defaults, and repayment history | Past payment performance is a central traditional risk signal. | **No for the applicant's own history** | The supplied tables contain no applicant-level prior account performance, days-past-due history, defaults, charge-offs, or repayment sequences. `TARGET` is a future/observed outcome label for the current training loan, not a predictor available at decision time. |
| Recent bureau inquiries / credit seeking | Recent inquiries can indicate active credit seeking and appear in the LendingClub study. | **Yes, directly** | `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_HOUR`, `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_DAY`, `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_WEEK`, `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_MON`, `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_QRT`, `AMT_REQ_CREDIT_BUREAU_YEAR` in both tables. These are counts by lookback window, not complete bureau histories. |
| Permissioned bank-account cash flow | Regulators highlight recurring income, fixed and variable expenses, account management over time, and residual balances as comparatively direct measures of repayment capacity for applicants with limited bureau data. | **No** | Neither table has bank transactions, deposit dates/amounts, expense categories, balance histories, overdrafts, nonsufficient-funds events, or end-of-period residual balances. `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` is a point-in-time stated amount and cannot substitute for a cash-flow time series. |
| Income regularity and volatility | Stable deposit patterns can reveal repayment capacity for gig workers or people with multiple income sources. | **No** | Requires dated deposits or repeated verified income observations. The data contains only `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL` and `NAME_INCOME_TYPE` at application time. |
| Expense burden and residual income | Fixed housing costs, variable expenses, and money left after obligations are key cash-flow measures named in the interagency statement. | **No** | No rent/mortgage payment, utilities, food/transport spending, recurring expense, account-balance, or residual-income fields are present. `NAME_HOUSING_TYPE` indicates tenure type but not housing expense. |
| Rent, utility, telecom, or other alternative bill-payment history | On-time recurring noncredit payments can add history when a bureau file is sparse. | **No** | No payment dates, amounts, delinquency flags, or histories for rent, utilities, mobile service, or subscriptions are supplied. `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_PHONE`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE`, and `FLAG_EMAIL` record contact availability/reachability, not payment performance. |
| Homeownership and housing situation | Homeownership is a traditional risk/control variable in the LendingClub evidence; housing tenure may also proxy for stability and fixed-cost structure. | **Yes, partially** | `FLAG_OWN_REALTY` and `NAME_HOUSING_TYPE` in both tables directly capture ownership/housing category. Property context is available through `HOUSETYPE_MODE`, `FONDKAPREMONT_MODE`, `WALLSMATERIAL_MODE`, `EMERGENCYSTATE_MODE`, and the normalized building fields listed below. No mortgage balance, rent/mortgage payment, equity, lien, or property value is present. |
| Residential property characteristics | Some application models use property condition and dwelling characteristics as stability/asset-context signals. | **Yes, directly as supplied normalized/categorical inputs** | In both tables: `APARTMENTS_AVG`, `BASEMENTAREA_AVG`, `YEARS_BEGINEXPLUATATION_AVG`, `YEARS_BUILD_AVG`, `COMMONAREA_AVG`, `ELEVATORS_AVG`, `ENTRANCES_AVG`, `FLOORSMAX_AVG`, `FLOORSMIN_AVG`, `LANDAREA_AVG`, `LIVINGAPARTMENTS_AVG`, `LIVINGAREA_AVG`, `NONLIVINGAPARTMENTS_AVG`, `NONLIVINGAREA_AVG`; the corresponding `_MODE` and `_MEDI` columns; plus `TOTALAREA_MODE`, `FONDKAPREMONT_MODE`, `HOUSETYPE_MODE`, `WALLSMATERIAL_MODE`, `EMERGENCYSTATE_MODE`. Their normalized scales are not translated to physical units in the dictionary. |
| Other assets and savings | Liquid reserves and owned assets can absorb income shocks; Upstart's disclosure gives applicant-reported savings as an example of a raw feature. | **Only weak asset proxies** | `FLAG_OWN_CAR`, `OWN_CAR_AGE`, and `FLAG_OWN_REALTY` in both tables indicate ownership. There is no savings balance, deposit/investment account balance, vehicle value, property value, equity, or lien information. A liquid-reserve feature cannot be built. |
| Education level and professional trajectory | Alternative lenders may use education and employment trajectory to supplement sparse bureau history; Upstart discloses education and employment information. | **Education level only; trajectory is incomplete** | `NAME_EDUCATION_TYPE` in both tables provides highest education category. The data lacks institution, area of study, grades, graduation date, prior jobs, promotions, and income history. `OCCUPATION_TYPE`, `ORGANIZATION_TYPE`, and `DAYS_EMPLOYED` provide only a current snapshot. |
| Local cost of living and economic conditions | Local unemployment, house prices, and cost of living can contextualize income and repayment capacity; local HPI and unemployment appear in the LendingClub study, and Upstart discloses local cost of living. | **Only coarse proxies** | `REGION_POPULATION_RELATIVE`, `REGION_RATING_CLIENT`, and `REGION_RATING_CLIENT_W_CITY` in both tables. The data has no geography identifier that can be joined to unemployment, house-price, or cost-of-living series. `data_map.md` also notes one test-table value of `-1` outside the documented 1–3 range for `REGION_RATING_CLIENT_W_CITY`. |
| Address stability and consistency | Address tenure/consistency and mismatches can support identity verification and stability assessment. | **Yes, partially** | `DAYS_REGISTRATION`, `REG_REGION_NOT_LIVE_REGION`, `REG_REGION_NOT_WORK_REGION`, `LIVE_REGION_NOT_WORK_REGION`, `REG_CITY_NOT_LIVE_CITY`, `REG_CITY_NOT_WORK_CITY`, and `LIVE_CITY_NOT_WORK_CITY` in both tables. There is no full address history or independent verification result. |
| Identity/contact verification and fraud indicators | Fraud and identity consistency are often assessed alongside credit risk because invalid applications contaminate repayment-risk decisions. Upstart's SEC filing describes third-party verification and fraud-model inputs. | **Yes, as limited application indicators** | `DAYS_ID_PUBLISH`, `DAYS_LAST_PHONE_CHANGE`, `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_EMP_PHONE`, `FLAG_WORK_PHONE`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE`, `FLAG_PHONE`, `FLAG_EMAIL`, and `FLAG_DOCUMENT_2` through `FLAG_DOCUMENT_21` in both tables. The dictionary does not identify what documents 2–21 are, and there are no device, IP, identity-vendor, or verified-income results. |
| Digital footprint and online application behavior | Peer-reviewed work finds that website access/registration footprints can complement bureau information for unbanked or thin-file applicants. | **Mostly no** | Only `WEEKDAY_APPR_PROCESS_START` and `HOUR_APPR_PROCESS_START` capture application timing. The data lacks device type, operating system, browser, IP/geolocation, email-domain quality, acquisition channel, session navigation, typing behavior, and other registration/access metadata studied as digital footprints. Timing alone should not be represented as a full digital-footprint feature set. |
| Applicant's social-network credit performance | Some alternative models may examine network signals, although these raise material privacy, explainability, proxy, and fair-lending concerns. | **Yes, as supplied aggregates** | `OBS_30_CNT_SOCIAL_CIRCLE`, `DEF_30_CNT_SOCIAL_CIRCLE`, `OBS_60_CNT_SOCIAL_CIRCLE`, `DEF_60_CNT_SOCIAL_CIRCLE` in both tables. These describe observed/default counts in the applicant's social surroundings, not the applicant's own payment history. Their provenance and construction are not documented sufficiently for production use. |
| Demographic/protected characteristics | These may correlate statistically with outcomes but are legally restricted or inappropriate as ordinary repayment-risk predictors in U.S. lending. | **Present, but do not treat as ordinary underwriting features** | `CODE_GENDER`, `DAYS_BIRTH`, `NAME_FAMILY_STATUS`, and `CNT_CHILDREN` are in both tables. Regulation B prohibits consideration of sex and marital status in evaluating creditworthiness, bars childbearing/childrearing assumptions, and permits age only under limited conditions. These fields may instead be needed for governance, bias testing, or legally reviewed uses. `data_map.md` also documents `XNA` gender and `Unknown` family-status sentinels in training. |
| Model outcome for supervised learning | A repayment outcome is needed to train and validate a default-risk model, but it is not an application-time feature. | **Yes for training only** | `application_train.TARGET` is the labeled payment-difficulty outcome. `application_test` has no `TARGET`. It must never be included among predictors because that would be target leakage. |

### Exact normalized residential-property columns

For completeness, the `_MODE` and `_MEDI` fields referenced above are:

- `_MODE`: `APARTMENTS_MODE`, `BASEMENTAREA_MODE`, `YEARS_BEGINEXPLUATATION_MODE`, `YEARS_BUILD_MODE`, `COMMONAREA_MODE`, `ELEVATORS_MODE`, `ENTRANCES_MODE`, `FLOORSMAX_MODE`, `FLOORSMIN_MODE`, `LANDAREA_MODE`, `LIVINGAPARTMENTS_MODE`, `LIVINGAREA_MODE`, `NONLIVINGAPARTMENTS_MODE`, `NONLIVINGAREA_MODE`.
- `_MEDI`: `APARTMENTS_MEDI`, `BASEMENTAREA_MEDI`, `YEARS_BEGINEXPLUATATION_MEDI`, `YEARS_BUILD_MEDI`, `COMMONAREA_MEDI`, `ELEVATORS_MEDI`, `ENTRANCES_MEDI`, `FLOORSMAX_MEDI`, `FLOORSMIN_MEDI`, `LANDAREA_MEDI`, `LIVINGAPARTMENTS_MEDI`, `LIVINGAREA_MEDI`, `NONLIVINGAPARTMENTS_MEDI`, `NONLIVINGAREA_MEDI`.

## Bottom line

The supplied application tables can support a conventional application-score feature set centered on stated income, current employment, requested loan structure, housing/asset indicators, recent bureau inquiries, coarse external scores, and application-verification signals. They **cannot** build the most directly useful thin-file alternative-data features identified by regulators: longitudinal cash flow, income regularity, expense burden, residual balances, or recurring bill-payment history. They also lack full bureau tradelines, so the data cannot define thin-file status or reconstruct an applicant's own credit history.

Any later modeling should keep three distinctions explicit:

1. A direct raw field is not the same as a validated underwriting feature.
2. A proxy such as housing type, region rating, or external score is not equivalent to the missing construct it resembles.
3. Legally restricted and sensitive fields should be governed separately from candidate repayment-risk predictors.
